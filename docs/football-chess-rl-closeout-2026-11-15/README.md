# Football Chess reinforcement learning research closeout

**Status: Closed on November 15, 2026.** The evidence supports ending the active Football Chess RL and benchmark research program at the pilot stage. The probability-aware planning pilot missed its registered improvement threshold. In the move-ranking pilot, scratch self-play produced no learning signal and RL fine-tuning failed to improve on imitation. Imitation's equal-node advantage did not carry into either equal-time repeat. The research records are preserved for use as a planning testbed. [Project status](status.json) · [Data and analysis notes](evidence/README.md)

## What makes the game useful

Football Chess combines several actions of buildup with an opposing keeper decision and a rule-defined scoring distribution. An early positioning move can change a later lane, receiving square, or feasible finish. Given a declared shot and legal keeper reply, the engine provides the exact conditional probability of a goal. A catch, rebound, or corner can change how the possession continues. That distinction makes it possible to test immediate shot estimates separately from longer decision sequences. Nine held-out positions met the shot pilot's criterion for buildup depth, six of them from natural play. Their six-action reference values exceeded the four-action values by 0.071 to 0.186 expected goals. This was measured on a bounded tree with shared Tactical ranking. [Literature and rules review](../football-chess-research-review-2026-11-14.md) · [Shot pilot](../shot-probability-pilot-2026-11-14/README.md)

Existing platforms already give researchers baselines for reinforcement learning, game search, and stochastic grid sports. OpenSpiel supports experiments across multiple games. MimicBot demonstrates imitation plus RL in the related Bot Bowl environment. Football Chess's narrower contribution is a discrete buildup problem paired with checkable probabilities for the shot after the keeper responds. The pilots tested whether this affordance or a learned shortlist gave enough additional value to justify an ongoing program. [OpenSpiel](https://arxiv.org/abs/1908.09453) · [MimicBot](https://arxiv.org/abs/2108.09478)

## Chance, decision depth and baseline search

- **Known shot probabilities vs sampled outcomes.** Across 33 held-out positions from 31 source families, the primary group comprised nine deeper buildup positions. Using the underlying shot probability lowered first-move regret by **0.000 expected goals** at both the 2 ms and 8 ms allowances after common tree construction. The registered target was a reduction of at least 0.005. The existing ranker had already put a reference-optimal first move at the top in eight of nine cases. Known probabilities did help the immediate goalkeeper control at the shorter budget, matching the analytic best reply in 88 of 88 calls vs 82 of 88 with sampling. The fixed-tree comparison isolates the shot backup and trains no policy. It provides no usable 2 ms timed comparison for the separate continuation-aware keeper experiment. [Shot pilot and decision records](../shot-probability-pilot-2026-11-14/README.md)
- **Frozen search controls.** The separate 1,600-game node ladder found that changing from 1,400 nodes and depth cap 8 to 5,600 nodes and depth cap 12 cost 0.152 xG per game in the 400-game head-to-head, with 383 vs 91 tackles conceded. The 95% seed-cluster interval for the xG difference was −0.175 to −0.129. Extra search work gave a weaker result for the tested evaluator and rankings. The ladder varied both node and depth caps, leaving the cause open. [Search sensitivity](../search-sensitivity-2026-11-13/README.md)
- **Earlier RL on Tactical's leaf evaluator.** An early seven-feature residual went 1–10–5 in 16 held-out segments with a 1–5 goal score. A later 37-feature experiment ended after 13 of 32 planned held-out scenarios, with a partial record of 0–12–1. Neither checkpoint met the evidence needed for promotion. [Early held-out evaluation](https://github.com/yuanrui3/Football-chess/blob/45fc3f4a43f2775c1eaf6e27737ef4aba76a7403/docs/selfplay-rl-2026-10-16/EVALUATION.md) · [Later training audit](https://github.com/yuanrui3/Football-chess/blob/45fc3f4a43f2775c1eaf6e27737ef4aba76a7403/docs/selfplay-hour-2026-10-16/RESULTS.md)

## Learned move ranking

The three studies comprise 168 held-out games against frozen Tactical, using the reviewed engine at [`45fc3f4`](https://github.com/yuanrui3/Football-chess/tree/45fc3f4a43f2775c1eaf6e27737ef4aba76a7403). Each arm used 24 games across three training runs with two fresh match seeds per run, paired by candidate color and kickoff. Each game had a 120-action horizon, with pending shots resolved. The 21-feature linear ranker used the same leaf evaluation and search settings as Tactical. Both equal-time studies reused the original imitation checkpoints and evaluated plain imitation and a variant that applies the learned ordering to Tactical's native defensive shortlist.

**Scratch RL ran out of useful outcomes.** In 384 training segments it sampled 61,440 actions, settled eight shots and scored zero goals. Every segment was a draw with no weight updates. Its held-out record describes a zero-weight ranker inside Tactical search. The imitation-initialized arm generated 118 goals and 45 updates across the same training budget. Those updates reduced the matched margin against frozen by 0.250 goals per game relative to imitation, with a 95% exploratory interval of −0.667 to +0.083. Its xG margin fell by 0.125 per game [−0.236, −0.031]. [Training and equal-node evidence](evidence/README.md)

**The equal-time tests did not reproduce the equal-node gain.** Plain imitation took 29% longer per search than frozen Tactical in its equal-node matchup. Under shared time allowances, its goals and W/D/L points tied frozen in both repeats. The defensive variant lost on realized goals at 200 ms and tied at 777.46 ms, the pooled original mean across 4,246 frozen Tactical searches. At 200 ms the defensive variant's matched xG margin rose by 0.050 per game relative to plain while its goal margin fell by 0.125. At 777.46 ms, its matched xG gain was 0.021 [−0.066, +0.145], mostly from one checkpoint, and its goal margin was level with plain [−0.250, +0.208]. Mean search times differed by at most 1.0 ms within a 200 ms matchup and 4.1 ms within a 777.46 ms matchup. The clocks were checked between search work units and allowed occasional overruns. ## Basis for closure

The current approach offers neither a demonstrated RL improvement nor a repeatable playing-strength gain at equal decision time. The bounded chance experiment also gives no performance reason to scale the original benchmark hypothesis. Football Chess retains value for small, auditable studies of buildup, shot probabilities and search failures. Its useful mechanics alone do not establish a reason to fund further RL training or benchmark development.

The evidence covers compact learners, short games, a single frozen opponent and three independently trained checkpoints per ranking arm. Realized goals were scarce. The two timed repeats evaluated imitation only, and comparisons across budgets also change the match seeds. The work leaves open richer learners, curricula that start from scoring opportunities, training on probability-aware targets, and stronger continuation-aware keeper search. The closeout is a decision about the active research program. The recorded results support reuse of the environment and its test positions for narrower questions.
