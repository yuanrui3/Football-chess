# Box access and knight finishing trial

The final rules are **B: every outfield piece may enter either box, with the ordinary knight goal probability (×1)**. The king cannot enter the opponent's box. The user removed the proposed knight doubling after the trial. This was a rule screen, not an AI strength comparison or a calibration to professional football.

This historical trial predates weighted movement, the later accuracy adjustment, fouls, reserved goal-mouth cells, the outside-to-box dribble/run ban and optional pass-and-run. Its all-outfield access selection remains subject to those later movement and occupancy restrictions; its observed rates describe the frozen trial builds.

All factorial variants share a 14 × 17 board, four-cell goal (files 5–8), six-by-three box (files 4–9), model 12 defender blocks, the stricter post-angle curve, central-origin bonus, the same pawn-front formation and one shared off-ball Pawn-or-King bonus move. Each defender intersecting an ordinary shot contributes a 45% block chance; a block goes out 85% of the time and otherwise gives the defender possession. Knights retain their lob/jump bypass. The experimental multiplier rescales all failed-outcome probability so the complete distribution sums to one. `constants.json` preserves the common geometry, model coefficients, formation and search budget.

Each row contains 16 Casual selfplay games: seeds 84100–84107, each with White and Black kickoffs, 480 atomic actions per game. All 469 declarations resolved; **none targeted the shooter's own goal**. No game exceeded four total goals. No invalid positions or failed probability checks appeared. B used 545 restart actions out of 7,680 (7.1%), with a longest consecutive corner chain of three and maximum exact-position repeat of two. It had three stalling awards and one scoreless game. These small, paired cohorts do not establish a precise scoring rate.

The knight bonus increased C's goals by 18% over control A and 56% over B. Its main effect was origin concentration: 37 of 39 goals inside the custom box, 25 from the endline, and 19 of its 24 knight goals from the endline. All seven unavoidable 100% finishes began at canonical (6,0). The cap was intentional, but the repeated finish was a balance concern. C was removed at the user's request; the results did not show a general score explosion.

The public harness retains legacy all-target `shots`/`xG` fields and adds explicit own/opponent fields.

No exact-position cycles or corner storms appeared in the Tactical screen. Its low shooting frequency is an unresolved pace limitation. The opportunity audit checked every release in all 12 games and found no rejected opponent-goal finish worth at least 25% after the best legal keeper response. This is evidence about attack construction and defensive pressure, not proof of realistic soccer pacing.

The selective Tactical search also has a known three-allowance offside-clearance weakness. With fresh allowances in the Black clearance fixture from `tests/box-offside.test.mjs`, it can choose B2→i8, R1→i4 and K→g17, leaving N1 on h2 offside inside Ivory's box. This was reproduced on the final B source; a subsequent teammate kick is correctly penalized. The focused clearance test explicitly isolates the case with only the off-ball move remaining. It does not guarantee successful clearance through every three-action plan.

The matched study predates two narrow fixes: AI collector eligibility now recognizes the free King move, and a king catching a **different teammate's own-goal-directed shot** must release immediately as a back-pass. Self-shot catches retain their previous behavior. The latter fix directly affects the two historical Tactical same-team saves; those games describe the earlier implementation. No Casual factorial attempt targeted its own goal, so that particular loophole was not used in its spatial results.
