When Should a Language Model Change Its Move? Paired Shot and Pass Decisions in Purdue Football Chess

Yuanrui Chen

In three paired-board studies, a defender moves one square and the model chooses from three actions. The rules engine evaluates every option. The edit changes the best action in half of each study’s pairs. A fourth study holds a partially visible layout fixed and varies the reliability of a scout report.

Original benchmark · 100 pairs

Shooting · 60 pairs, 120 boards
Choose a declared target or disguise the shot. The goalkeeper responds to minimize the chance of a goal. The release includes coordinates and rules, prompts adding legal goalkeeper replies, and prompts adding the scoring odds for each reply.
Passing · 40 pairs, 80 boards
Choose a teammate for a pass that will reach its receiver. The attack holds its next move. The best pass maximizes the probability of retaining the ball after the defender’s next challenge.

What the paired scores show

Solving a pair requires picking the engine’s best action on both boards. On shots from coordinates and rules, Gemini 3.1 Flash-Lite solved 12 of 60 pairs and a geometric shot-line rule solved 52. On passes, Gemini and a rule based on tackle shapes and ranges each solved 38 of 40 pairs. The scores describe one recorded response per input on deliberately selected positions. Full-match play and the models’ training histories fall outside these measurements.

When is the knight a good pass?

Matched-image passing follow-up · 16 boards in eight fresh pairs
The author and Gemini saw the same pitch image, coordinates, rules and pass menu. The author solved seven pairs and Gemini solved two. Gemini chose the knight on 13 of 16 boards, although it was optimal on four. The author was familiar with the task. Open the 16-board quiz.
Cue reliability · 48 text decisions on six layouts
A scout reported whether an ordinary receiver was safe. The prompt supplied the tackle outcomes. A second receiver, either the rook or the knight, was protected and retained possession with probability 0.75. A 90%-reliable “safe” report favored the ordinary pass. The same report at 60% favored the protected pass.

Gemini made all 12 predicted reversals, six with each protected piece. It chose 24 knights and 24 rooks and maximized expected retention on all 48 decisions. Choosing the ordinary receiver only on a 90%-reliable “safe” report reproduces those choices. This leaves open how Gemini infers who can be tackled on the fully specified boards.

Reproduce the results

From benchmarks/paired-positions/ in a checkout of the expanded dataset, run the following with Node.js 24.2 or later. The scorers use the included files and run offline.

node anc/score.mjs
node anc/pass/reproduce.mjs
node anc/native/verify.mjs
node anc/followup/reproduce.mjs
node anc/followup/value-of-information.mjs
node anc/cue/reproduce.mjs

Inspect the shot scorer, geometry baseline, pass scorer and engine verification script. For the additional studies, use the 16-board scorer, value-of-information analysis and cue experiment scorer.
